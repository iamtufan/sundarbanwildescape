When to Experience the Sundarbans

Each season paints the world’s largest mangrove forest with distinct wildlife movements and moods.

Peak Wildlife Window

Winter: October – March

Balmy daytime temperatures (15°C–25°C), crisp morning mist, and minimal rainfall. Prime season for estuarine crocodiles basking on low-tide mudflats, migratory raptors, and deer herds at sweet-water holes.

Recommended for: First-time travelers, families, general birding
Dramatic Atmosphere

Monsoon: July – September

The rivers swell, the mangrove canopy turns into an electric neon green, and dramatic monsoon rain clouds sweep across vast estuarine horizons. High rainfall, comfortable vessel cabins, and intense sensory solitude.

Recommended for: Nature poets, rain enthusiasts, landscape artists
Wild Harvest & Mudflats

Summer: April – June

Warmer days (28°C–36°C) coinciding with the traditional Mawali wild honey harvesting season. Animals congregate closely around interior sweet-water watchtower basins for hydration.

Recommended for: Serious wildlife photographers, cultural ethnographers
