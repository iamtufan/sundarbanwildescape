The Delta Ecology

Ten thousand square kilometers where freshwater and sea intertwine.

Formed by the mighty silt deposits of the Ganga, Brahmaputra, and Meghna rivers flowing into the Bay of Bengal, the Indian Sundarbans represents the largest contiguous mangrove forest on Earth. Here, towering stilt-roots of Sundari (Heritiera fomes) and Gewa withstand tidal shifts exceeding four meters twice every single day.

This is not a manicured sanctuary. It is an amphibious wilderness governed entirely by the moon and the tides. As an accredited ecotourism operator, Sundarban Wild Escape bridges this wild frontier with uncompromising comfort, profound local reverence, and complete ecological accountability.

Fauna of the Sundarban Tiger Reserve

Wild Inhabitants of the Mangrove Maze

We observe wildlife strictly under West Bengal Forest Department ethical protocols. We never bait, chase, or disrupt animals. Every sighting is genuine, wild, and hard-earned by tracking low-tide signs and forest acoustics.

Apex Predator • Endangered

Royal Bengal Tiger

Panthera tigris tigris

Adapted to drink saline estuarine water and swim across tidal channels up to 5 kilometers wide. Tracking relies on reading fresh pugmarks on low-tide mudbanks.

Sightings are authentic and never guaranteed.
Apex Aquatic Predator

Estuarine Saltwater Crocodile

Crocodylus porosus

The world's largest living reptile. Frequently seen warming itself on exposed silt banks during low-tide winter sun between November and February.

Commonly spotted on Sudhanyakhali & Netidhopani banks.
Vital Prey Base

Spotted Chital Deer

Axis axis

Graze in gentle herds on tender mangrove shoots and freshwater vegetation. Their sharp alarm calls notify naturalists of nearby predators.

Abundant around Sajnekhali & Dobanki freshwater hides.
9 Delta Species

Sundarban Kingfishers

Alcedinidae Family

Includes the rare Brown-winged, Black-capped, Collared, Ruddy, and Pied kingfishers hunting crabs and mudskippers among pneumatophores.

Active in narrow creeks of Pirkhali & Chokhakhali.
Aquatic Mammals

Gangetic & Irrawaddy Dolphins

Platanista gangetica / Orcaella brevirostris

Surface gracefully where sweet river discharge collides with marine tides, particularly at the expansive Panchamukhani confluence.

Observed during calm morning boat drifts.
Semi-Aquatic Giant

Water Monitor Lizard

Varanus salvator

Growing over two meters in length, these impressive monitor lizards forage along the tangled root line and swim with remarkable agility.

Frequently observed sunning on fallen mangrove logs.

The Passage from Kolkata to the Silent Creeks

Your expedition begins long before you board the riverboat. Leaving behind the bustling metropolis of Kolkata, you journey southeast through South 24 Parganas, witnessing emerald paddy fields, freshwater bheries, and winding rural embankments.

1

Kolkata Departure

Chauffeured pickup from Airport (CCU), Howrah/Sealdah stations, or your city hotel.

2

Scenic Drive via Canning to Godkhali Ghat

Approx. 85 km (2.5 to 3 hours). The historic road terminus where the roads end and tidal waters begin.

3

Embarkation onto Safari Cruiser

Board our private wooden tourist vessel. Glide across the Gomor and Bidya rivers into the Tiger Reserve.
